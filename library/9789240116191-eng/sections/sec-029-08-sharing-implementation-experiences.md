# 08. Sharing implementation experiences

## COUNTRY: RWANDA

| Aspect | REGULATORY | DATA | TECHNOLOGY | PEOPLE | CHANGE MANAGEMENT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Status** | • Regulations governing the implementation of identification data capture and data sharing for traceability of pharmaceutical product<br>• Guidelines for identification and labelling of pharmaceutical products published in Aug 2022<br>• Guidelines for product and location master data sharing published in Aug 2022<br>• The National Vision & Strategy for Pharmaceutical Traceability Leveraging GS1 Global Standards, published in 2019 | 7134 products are uploaded into NPC and 2611 (H) are mapped with GTINs | NPC hosted on open-source PCMT, and data sharing mechanism piloted through health information exchange mediator | Catalog manager onboarded to centrally manage product master data in NPC | • Establishment of a pharmaceutical traceability TWG to lead the strategy, collaboration and oversight of traceability implementation<br>• Rwanda trade item numbers defined for products without GTIN |
| **Process** | A workshop with stakeholders, including Ministry of Health, Rwanda Food and Drugs Authority, Rwanda Medical Supply Ltd., and implementation partners, was held to define master data guidelines | • Data collected manually from packages as a short-term approach<br>• Data to be collected formally through registration process | • Open-source PCMT tailored for country needs<br>• NPC being integrated with other systems through mediator | Detailed job descriptions defined for catalog manager part of Rwanda FDA team | • SOPs with transition details defined<br>• Mapping between old and new product and trade item ids established |
| **Challenges** | Time-intensive process to establish and release guidelines | Obtaining high-quality data from sources like manufacturers | Integrating NPC data with other consuming systems necessitating additional modifications to those systems | Developing human resource capabilities to manage system administration and enhancements of NPC | • Quantity conversions across product packaging hierarchies<br>• Managing both products with and without GTINs |
| **Approaches adopted** | Engaged government stakeholders, donors, partners, and the private sector, including manufacturers and distributors, through an inclusive approach | • Started with a minimal set of product attributes<br>• Identified alternate data sources like labels on product packaging | Adopted phased pilot-based approach to gradually integrate NPC with other systems | • Collaborated with implementation partners and donors for technical support<br>• Developing long-term strategies to build capacity | • NPC configured to maintain packaging hierarchies with quantity conversion factors<br>• A local attribute is used to identify trade items without GTINs |
| **Best-practices/lessons learnt** | Engage all relevant stakeholders throughout the process | • Standardize national product codes across all products<br>• Develop processes to map local product IDs to GTINs<br>• Designate NPC as the national source of all product master data | Use an information mediator such as OpenHIM to integrate NPC with other systems. This minimizes the need for point-to-point integrations | Assign the catalog manager to oversee all product master data management activities, including adding and updating products | Design NPC, data management processes, and attributes to facilitate a seamless transition to GTIN use as they become available, without disrupting ongoing transactions |

## COUNTRY: ZAMBIA

| Aspect | REGULATORY | DATA | TECHNOLOGY | PEOPLE | CHANGE MANAGEMENT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Status** | 2023: Zambia Medicines Regulatory Authority publishes enforceable Guidelines for Traceability (Trade Item and Location Identification) | • 7414 pharmaceutical products harmonized with 551 GTINs collected | • 2021: National Product Catalog (NPC) implemented through Product Catalog Management Tool (PCMT)<br>• 2022: PCMT integrated with Global Data Synchronisation Network to source from suppliers and linked with Warehouse Management System, provide master data to Zambia Medicines and Medical Supplies Agency<br>• 2024: WMS Expert integrated with eLMIS<br>• 2024: WMS Expert Portal configured to support serialization<br>• 2024: Scanning of GS1 barcodes introduced at ZAMMSA receiving functions<br>• 2024: Mobile App deployed to scan serial numbers | Catalog managers identified and placed responsible for managing | 2024: products from two suppliers tracked from manufacturer to ZAMMSA to a Health Facility |
| **Process** | • Collaborative workshop to launch the Zambia pharmaceutical traceability strategy through collective action of government, private sector, and development partner stakeholders<br>• 2024, Zambia begun to implement a proof of concept (POC) that is fast-tracking serialization | • Manual collection of data, manually collect serial numbers from scanning labels products | • Open-source Product Catalog Management Tool (PCMT) adopted<br>• Incorporated into the country’s Health Information Exchange framework through SMART Zambia<br>• Mobile App deployed to scan serial numbers from packs | • Health workers trained to authenticate medicines before dispensing to patients using serial numbers (3 facilities) | • National Pharmaceutical Traceability Strategy formulated, and clear objectives set<br>• Enhanced stock visibility within the ZAMMSA Warehouse Management System (WMS)<br>• Scanning of GS1 barcodes piloted at 3 health facilities to dispense medicines |
| **Challenges** | • Limited resources and legal framework, governance structure and resources to operationalize the strategy | • Barcode scanning relied on a paper-based and semi-automated system of inventory | Conflict of interest from other partners who had alternative operators | • Attrition and unavailability of trained HR to manage and drive the system | • GS1, being a new concept, presented a barrier as it required effective change management and addressing perception challenges |
| **Approaches adopted** | • Phased approach to implementation to align with national guidelines<br>• Technical working group established to review progress | • Continuous product data harmonization update | • Leveraging on the available and existing technology<br>• Technical design specifications and procurement of barcode scanners | Trained catalog managers responsible for managing and maintaining this data | Regular stakeholder engagement |
| **Best-practices/lessons learnt** | Stakeholder engagement | Standardized | PCMT has allowed harmonized product identifiers across the supply chain | Catalog managers placed responsible for managing | Improved collaboration |

## COUNTRY: NIGERIA

| Aspect | REGULATORY | DATA | TECHNOLOGY | PEOPLE | CHANGE MANAGEMENT |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Status** | • Regulatory Guidelines for traceability includes requirements for identification and labelling of pharmaceutical products. Regulatory Guidelines Draft published in 2022 for inputs by stakeholders. The guidelines are in the final stages of preparation to be gazetted by the Ministry of Justice of Nigeria.<br>• Guidelines for product and location master data sharing published in April 2022 | 6517 products are uploaded into NPC and 794 of them are mapped with GTINs | NPC hosted a Product Catalogue Management Tool (PCMT) for data sharing mechanism pulled from NAFDAC Database, GDSN and GS1 Nigeria One Source, a GTIN issuance system/Catalogue used in Nigeria to populate the PCMT | Catalog manager onboarded to centrally manage product master data in NPC | National Agency for Food and Drug Administration and Control registration number is defined for all products including products without GTINs. Mandatory Traceability pilots for Narcotic and Controlled substances are ongoing with requirements to obtain GTINs and upload the 29 mandatory Master data Attributes. International agencies are mandating for GTINs and Master Data as part of their procurement processes. |
| **Process** | A series of meetings with stakeholders, including NAFDAC, GS1 Local and Global Office Healthcare teams, and implementation partners, was held to define master data guidelines | • Data collected manually from packages as a short-term approach for pilots only<br>• Data to be collected formally through registration process and upon GTIN registration by Brand owners/MAHs for GTINs issued locally in Nigeria and via GDSN for non-GS1 Nigeria issued GTINs | • GS1 Nigeria One Source is used as PCMT tailored for country needs for GTINs issued in Nigeria<br>• NPC being integrated with other systems through GDSN for GTINs not issued in Nigeria<br>• NAFDAC Traceability Information System (TIS) is integrated with NPC to hold MD for all regulated products (food, drugs, medical devices, etc.) | Detailed job descriptions defined for catalog manager part of NAFDAC team | • SOPs with transition details defined<br>• Mapping between old and new product and trade item ids established |
| **Challenges** | Time-intensive process to establish and release guidelines | Obtaining high-quality data from sources like manufacturers | Integrating NPC data with other consuming systems necessitating additional modifications to those systems | Developing human resource capabilities to manage system administration and enhancements of NPC | • Quantity conversions across product packaging hierarchies<br>• Managing both products with and without GTINs |
| **Approaches adopted** | Engaged government stakeholders, donors, partners, and the private sector, including manufacturers and distributors, through an inclusive approach | • Started with a minimal set of product attributes<br>• Identified alternate data sources like labels on product packages | Adopted phased pilot-based approach to gradually integrate NPC with other systems | • Collaborated with GS1 and donors for technical support<br>• Developing long-term strategies to build capacity | • NPC configured to maintain packaging hierarchies with quantity conversion factors<br>• NAFDAC registration Number is used to identify trade items without GTIN regulatory enforcement across all product categories |
| **Best-practices/lessons learnt** | Engage all relevant stakeholders throughout the process | • Standardize national product codes<br>• Develop data structure to map products to GTINs<br>• Designate NPC as the national source of all product master data | Use a key issuance system and GDSN as an information mediator to integrate NPC with other systems. This minimizes the need for point-to-point integrations | Assign the catalog manager to oversee all product master data management activities, including adding and updating products | Design NPC, data management processes, and attributes to facilitate a seamless transition to GTIN use as they become available, without disrupting ongoing transactions |
