What a folio is

The knowledge graph is four sub-graphs, and a layer declares which of them it holds.

Content is what somebody authors and a reader reads — this layer’s subject. Skills are the instructions an agent follows to do the work. Processes are the BPMN diagrams that say who does what, in which order, and where the gates are — they are executable, not illustrations. Tools are what an agent can call.

Getting anything in is acquisition: unstructured and semi-structured sources arrive through uploads/ or through the conversation, and are ingested into library/. It is a step rather than a graph typology of its own — there is no fifth directory to look for.

Scope: the content model — what a folio IS. Not the skills and tools that operate on it, and not any particular folio’s chapters.

  • Content A document is structured prose; a paper adds the block kinds whose assertion is a formal claim.
  • Skills Every skill and role, and how they fit together.
  • Processes The BPMN diagrams, which are the source of truth rather than a picture of it.
  • Tools What an agent can call, served over MCP.

What this site is

  • The IRIS-style home — IRIS’s home page, rendered from this repository’s catalogue and themed as IRIS, at this site’s root. Each held item links to its library entry page; communities and collections go to the catalogue page. It is an ingested copy: not WHO, and not live.
  • The catalogue — what the catalogue knows, and what it records that it does not know. Every node declares whether its bytes are here (materialized), elsewhere (referenced) or unestablished (unknown), and there is no default.
  • The gap is the point. IRIS reports its own figures for items and files; the catalogue holds a few and models the rest by reference. The census is bun run check:catalogue, never a number written into a page.

This repository is the development home of the instance; an official copy is expected to live under WHO’s own account.
