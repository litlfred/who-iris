Diagram Interchange is not optional

A .bpmn with no DI section — no explicit x/y on its shapes — parses and renders blank, although the XML looks complete.

bun run cat render:bpmn renders every diagram to docs/assets/img/workflows/ with bpmn-js in headless Chromium; bun run cat render:bpmn:check fails when an SVG is stale. Run the renderer and look at the SVG before you call a diagram done.

Readable at 100%: check it before and after (author check)

Every diagram you write or touch must, when you are done:

  • fit 1400 × 900 in diagram units, so its 16px text reads at about 14px in a 1200px band without zooming; every task a fixed 120 × 120 square: boxes never grow to fit their names, and a name that does not fit is shortened (TASK_SIZE in the tools layer);
  • have no label on another label, on a flow (its own included), on a box or across a lane line: a gateway’s question above the diamond, its answers beside their flows, every label inside one lane;
  • have right-angled flows and every name inside its box, pool and lane names inside their strips;
  • have no crossing flows, and the lane order that keeps flows between lanes shortest.

bun run cat bpmn:detangle <file> reports all of it, and --write fixes what it can (lane order, right angles, box and lane sizes, label places). Run it before and after, and put both counts in the PR.

Zero findings on every diagram you touch, or it does not merge (owner, 2026-11-02, after 63 of 80 harness diagrams were found with findings: “improve bpmn authoring process/skills so doesnt happen again”). A report nobody has to act on is how they piled up. Run bpmn:detangle <file> --check last; a finding you leave needs a reason in the PR, and only diagram-too-wide / lane-order may be left that way. Then look at the render: the check cannot yet see a box drawn across a lane line or outside its own lane, two flows drawn on top of each other, or a flow that wanders through a lane it does not stop in. Those are found only by looking. The same findings are in render:bpmn’s warnings and the process’s QA report, so a reviewer sees what the author left. The procedure, and why the budget is 1400 × 900, is bpmn-detangle.

In this repository, a diagram is executable

processes/**/*.bpmn are not pictures. workflow_start / workflow_next / workflow_complete run them, and workflow_complete refuses a step that is not enabled. That has consequences for how you author:

  • Every activity carries <bootstrap.processes:skill ref="…"/> naming the skill that implements it, and <cat-harness.processes:bean store="beans/"/> where it touches the work plan. bun run cat check:workflow-refs fails on a ref that resolves to nothing, and bun run cat kg:audit additionally fails when the named skill exists but no package can serve it.
  • Every lane names a role. Declare the role once as a top-level <bpmn:resource id="<role id>" name="<title>"/> (before the collaboration and the process), and set partitionElementRef="<role id>" on the lane; the id is the role’s in scenarios/roles.json, verbatim. Lane names are free text; an explicit ref is the join that does not depend on spelling. <bootstrap.processes:role ref> is the deprecated form. A diagram using any bootstrap.processes: element declares the namespace as the first child of <bpmn:definitions>: <bpmn:import namespace="https://litlfred.github.io/bootstrap/v0/processes/ns/" location="https://litlfred.github.io/bootstrap/v0/processes/ns.jsonld" importType="http://www.w3.org/ns/json-ld"/>.
  • A store is not a lane. Lanes are for people and agents, the roles that act. A place where things are kept, such as the work plan (work items), a corpus, a session record, an activity log or a published bucket, is a <bpmn:dataStoreReference>. The tasks that read or write it point at it with data associations, and each task sits in the lane of whoever does it. A “Work plan — beans” lane holding “Open the bean” says the store does the work, when the agent does.
  • The process’s own bpmn:documentation opens with what it is for. Its first sentence is the diagram’s row on the publication-workflow page. kg-export carries it on the Process node as summary (the whole text as description), the page reads it from the published named-subgraph JSON-LD (bun run cat subgraph:jsonld) rather than from anything written by hand, and check:process-index fails when a declared diagram’s node has none. So that sentence says what the process does or answers — not who asked for it, which work item or issue it came from, or where the work got to. Those belong later in the documentation, or in the work item.
  • A gateway may be computed rather than chosen — see dmn-authoring.
  • Policy is declared on the process. <cat-harness.processes:policy enforcement="strict"/> means workflow_gate refuses a step that is not enabled; absent policy means strict. Steps marked relaxable="false" may never be relaxed by any package.

Readable diagrams: what the research supports

These rules are what holds up from the BPMN comprehension research, checked mechanically. kg:audit reports each rule below per process (src/workflow/diagram-quality.ts in the tools layer). The sources, and how strong each rule’s evidence is, are in the process-model-quality methodology.

  • Separate routing from work. A task never has two outgoing flows: put an exclusive or parallel gateway after it, or a reader must guess fork vs choice (activity-implicit-split, major). A loop-back INTO a task is fine. A gateway either joins or splits, not both (gateway-mixed). A question is a gateway, never a task: a box named “Should it leave now?” or “Separate this graph?” is a decision drawn as work. If someone must judge first, draw that as a task (“Judge whether it should leave”) or a call to Process_AskThePerson, then the question on the gateway after it.
  • Keep it small. Split by phase into call activities past 20 flow nodes (process-size). That 20 is house style. The only threshold with evidence is 7PMG’s 50 (process-decompose, major), and it predicts modelling errors such as deadlocks, not reading speed.
  • One start event per process level (process-single-start). Several named end events are fine, and the work item CLOSE pattern needs them.
  • A decision is a question, its branches are the answers (decision-label-question).
  • Label activities verb-object: imperative verb, then its object (Review the draft, not Draft review) (activity-label-verb-first). The evidence is one study of how ambiguous labels felt (29 students), so this is reported, never gated.
  • A name says the step, nothing else. No trailing [skill] slug: the skill is in <bootstrap.processes:skill ref>, and the page links it from there. No step numbers (“1 ·”, “Phase 2:”, “P1.1”): the flows already give the order, and a number in front breaks verb-first. A number that cites a section of a source document goes in the task’s documentation. A list of numbered steps is a sign the diagram copies a document’s outline; group the steps into phases instead.

Split by phase: how

A diagram can be right in every step and still unreadable on its page. When process-size fires, or a diagram has a start event per lane, split it like this:

  • The top-level process shows the phases. Each phase is a <bpmn:callActivity calledElement="…"> to its own process. Keep at the top only what moves the work between phases: who hands off to whom, and the decisions that send it back (rejected → revise). A loop that crosses phases is drawn at the top; a loop inside a phase stays in that phase.
  • A phase lives in its own file, <parent>-<phase>.bpmn, next to the parent, with its own process id (Process_<Parent><Phase>) and a first documentation sentence that says what the phase does. It becomes the phase’s row on the publication-workflow page like any other diagram’s.
  • A phase that ends two ways ends in two named end events, and the caller’s gateway right after the call reads which one it reached, as GW_BeanYours reads Process_BeanOpen’s.
  • Keep the node ids when you move nodes into a phase. A test, a schema or a skill that points at Task_X then changes only its file and process, not its id.
  • Open and close the work item at the top, once. A process that is only ever called runs on its caller’s work item, so a phase calls neither Process_BeanOpen nor Process_BeanClose (check:bean-calls). “Once” holds for the top as well: a process with several outcomes routes them all into one Process_BeanClose call, which decides itself whether the work item is completed, scrapped or blocked, and only then branches to its named end events. One close per outcome repeats the same box and makes the diagram wide.
  • A caller’s adjudication accepts looks one level down. If the judgement moves into a phase, put accepts on the call to that phase, and take it off any caller further up.
  • Name a call activity for the phase, verb-object like any activity (“Review the change”), not for its file.

A phase that only one parent calls is still worth its own file: the point is the page a reader opens, not reuse.

Untangle every diagram you touch

The same discipline as ArchiMate views (archimate-models §”Untangle every view you touch”), and the same geometry: a change never adds a tangle. Run bun run cat kg:audit before and after, and compare edge-crossing, edge-through-node and edge-rectilinear for the diagram. Say both counts in the PR. Route loop-backs in the channel below the lane (bpmn-processes §”Edge routing”), then look at the rendered SVG.

What the evidence does NOT support

Do not import these from style guides or model output; each was checked:

  • Mandatory left-to-right flow. Figl & Strembeck 2015 (44 people) found no significant difference in accuracy or time across four directions. Lay out left-to-right for consistency, not because it reads faster.
  • A word cap on labels, a gateway fan-out cap of 4, and a fixed colour palette. No study behind them. Colouring matching split and join gateways made novices more accurate and experts faster, not more accurate (Reijers et al. 2011).
  • OR-gateways need no rule: the process model refuses inclusive gateways at load.

If it has actors, activities and a control flow, it is BPMN

Not a Mermaid fence. Mermaid stays for the things that are not processes — component maps, lattices, navigation graphs. The audit of which is which is on the publication workflow page, which the content core authors.

Why this skill is in a platform package

It covers both a DAK’s L2 business processes and this repository’s own processes/**/*.bpmn, and platform processes such as crdm-requirements.bpmn name it, so it cannot live in a content-type package: a platform process depending on a content-type package crosses the boundary the platform-boundary-guard subagent keeps. Its DAK material stays here rather than in a second skill, because a generic skill naming a domain worked example is ordinary, and two skills that must be read together to author one diagram are not.

When a layer above transforms it

A layer built on this one may transform an authored business process into another artefact — FHIR Shorthand, for instance — and then the BPMN is a source artefact rather than documentation of one. Author it knowing it will be transformed: names and ids you choose here appear in what is generated. That layer’s own tooling skill has the mechanics; this one names no layer above it.
